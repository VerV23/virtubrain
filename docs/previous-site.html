Insights

Three ideas behind the work.

The verifiability gradient

How much autonomy an agent gets should follow how easily its work can be checked. A reconciliation either ties to the ledger or it doesn't, so an agent can own it. A provision depends on judgment, so a person signs it. Most processes contain both kinds of step, which is why blanket rules about AI in finance fail in practice.

Token-bound capacity

Shared services has always scaled by headcount: more volume, more seats. Agent capacity scales with compute. That changes how a service center prices work, plans peaks such as quarter-end, and writes service levels. Leaders who keep planning in seats will under-use what they have already paid for.

The continuous close

Month-end is a batch because checking used to be expensive. When agents reconcile and match every day, with evidence attached, most of the close is done before the period ends. What remains for the last days is the part that deserves a controller's attention: judgment, explanation and sign-off.
